# Learning methods behind the skill

These findings inform the teaching approach. Applying them to an AI-led repository walkthrough is a design inference, not a directly validated intervention. Learners differ in prior knowledge; adapt support to demonstrated understanding rather than assigning a fixed learning style. No method guarantees understanding, retention, or transfer to every project.

## Evidence and practical use

| Method | Evidence | Application in a study session | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval practice | Dunlosky et al. rate practice testing highly across the conditions reviewed. | After an explanation, invite the learner to reconstruct the flow or predict a result before showing the answer, then give feedback. | A correct answer to one question does not establish durable mastery. |
| Distributed practice | The same review rates practice spread over time highly. | Suggest revisiting a few questions in a later study session. | There is no universal best interval; a suggested review is not an authorized reminder. |
| Self-explanation | Bisra et al. synthesize evidence that prompts to explain relationships and causal steps can improve learning. | Ask why a boundary or step exists, then compare the explanation with the actual implementation. | Effects depend on the task and prompting; unsupported explanations need correction. |
| Worked examples and practice | The IES guide recommends alternating worked solutions with problem solving. | Trace a real request first, then ask the learner to predict a changed case. Reduce scaffolding when they demonstrate understanding. | Guidance useful for an unfamiliar mechanism may be redundant for an experienced learner. |
| Concrete and abstract representations | The IES guide recommends connecting concrete examples with abstract concepts. | Move from a real code path to its general principle, then to a different context and a counterexample. | Similar surface details do not prove the same principle applies. |
| Graphics with explanation | The IES guide recommends combining graphics and verbal descriptions. | Use a labeled flow or sequence diagram alongside an explanation of the same mechanism. | Decorative visuals and invented numerical charts add no evidence of learning. |

The overview, layered sections, and coverage route are practical choices for keeping a complex walkthrough navigable. They are not a scientifically established optimal lesson length or sequence. The default is a complete explanation at the learner's own pace; optional exercises or a requested guided session add opportunities to explain and apply ideas.

## Sources

- [Dunlosky et al. (2013), Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266). Review of ten techniques; practice testing and distributed practice receive high utility ratings, while self-explanation receives a moderate rating with evidence gaps. This is broad learning research, not a study of coding assistants.
- [Bisra et al. (2018), Inducing Self-Explanation: a Meta-Analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x). Synthesis of self-explanation prompting across instructional conditions. Its results do not establish that every conversational teach-back prompt works equally well.
- [Pashler et al. (2007), Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1). IES practice guide with recommendations and evidence ratings for spacing, worked examples, explanatory questions, graphics, and concrete/abstract connections. Recommendations have different evidence strengths and should not be presented as equally certain.

Sources reviewed on 2026-10-29. Recheck original publications when making stronger claims or changing the teaching approach; do not treat this short reference as a systematic review.
